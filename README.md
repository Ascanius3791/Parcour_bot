# Parcour bot

A physically simulated humanoid that learns parkour with reinforcement learning:
stairs, hurdles, balance beams, swing bars, walls with holes and platforms,
assembled into randomised obstacle corridors so the bot generalises instead of
memorising one course.

Physics: [MuJoCo](https://mujoco.org) (C++ API). Training: PyTorch (planned).

## Quick start (Linux / WSL2)

```bash
scripts/setup_deps.sh                 # downloads MuJoCo 3.17.0 into third_party/
cmake -S . -B build -G Ninja
cmake --build build
build/parcour_selftest                # physics sanity checks + speed benchmark
build/parcour_viewer                  # interactive 3D viewer on a generated course
```

Viewer options: `--seed N`, `--difficulty 0..1`, `--obstacles N`, `--section K`
(start at the K-th section), `--overview` (camera on the whole course). Pass a
scene file instead, e.g. `build/parcour_viewer assets/scene_flat.xml`, to get the
plain floor.

The viewer needs a display (WSLg on Windows 11 works out of the box). Build
without it via `-DPARCOUR_BUILD_VIEWER=OFF`.

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| Space / Right | pause / single step |

| D, + / - | drop from height (default 2 m), change height |
| M | controller: limp or hold pose |
| G | close / open both hands |
| I | invincible (joints don't break) |
| C / L / S | com + momentum arrows / per-limb momentum / stress colours |
| T | tracking camera on/off |
| N / P | next / previous course (seed) |
| [ / ] | difficulty down / up (0.1 steps) |
| J / K | start at the next / previous obstacle |
| V | camera overview of the whole course |
| R | route, checkpoints and progress marker |
| Mouse | left drag rotate, right drag pan, scroll zoom |
| Double-click, then Ctrl+drag | select a body, then rotate (left) or pull (right) it |

Yellow arrow: total linear momentum. Blue arrow: angular momentum about the
centre of mass (red sphere). Segments turn red as the joint above them
approaches its breaking point and magenta once it breaks.

## The humanoid

Generated by [`tools/gen_humanoid.py`](tools/gen_humanoid.py) into
`assets/humanoid.xml` (1.75 m, 70 kg; regenerate with `--height` / `--mass`).

* 18 rigid segments (pelvis, abdomen, chest, head, upper arms, forearms, hands,
  thighs, shanks, shoe-wearing feet, toes) plus 5 fingers per hand.
* Segment masses after de Leva (1996), lengths from standard anthropometry.
* 37 motor-driven hinge joints with human ranges of motion (knees and elbows
  bend one way only) and human torque limits, asymmetric where muscles are
  (e.g. knee extension 280 Nm, flexion 150 Nm).
* Each hand has 15 passive finger joints driven by one *grip* tendon actuator.
* 39 actions in [-1, 1], mapped onto the torque ranges.

### Joint injuries

[`src/core/injury.h`](src/core/injury.h) — every joint between two segments
breaks (and the episode ends) when, low-pass filtered over 10 ms,

* the reaction force through the joint, or
* the torque around axes the joint can't rotate about (bending a knee sideways), or
* the torque pushing the joint past its range (hyperextension; over-flexion is
  more tolerated, as soft tissue cushions it in a real body)

exceeds that joint's strength. The thresholds are rough values for an athletic
adult and are tunable.

### Sensors

[`src/core/body_state.h`](src/core/body_state.h) computes at every control step:
whole-body centre of mass, its velocity, total linear and angular momentum; for
each segment its position relative to the com, orientation, angular velocity,
linear momentum and angular momentum about the com; joint angles and
velocities, grip state, foot contact forces, head accelerometer/gyro and joint
stress. `BodyState::flatten` turns this into a 438-value, heading-invariant
policy input.

## Obstacle courses

[`src/core/course.h`](src/core/course.h) generates a corridor from a seed and a
difficulty in [0, 1]: a start runway, N obstacle sections and a finish runway.
The corridor turns by up to 25° between sections and floats over an abyss, so
leaving it sideways or missing a jump is a fall.

| Obstacle | Easy (difficulty 0) | Hard (difficulty 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Stairs (up or down) | 2–3 steps, 10–12 cm | up to 8 steps, 10–22 cm |
| Hurdle (board or vault box) | 15–20 cm | up to 1 m high, 0.9 m deep |
| Wall with a hole | hole 1.0–1.2 m wide, 1.2–1.3 m tall, at the floor | hole down to 0.65 × 0.7 m, up to 0.9 m above the floor |
| Balance beam over a pit | 45–60 cm wide, 1.5–2 m long | 12–25 cm wide, up to 5 m long |
| Swing bars over a pit | 1 bar at 2.0 m, 1–1.4 m pit | up to 3 bars at 2.0–2.4 m, pit up to 6.4 m |
| Platforms (pillars with gaps) | 2 pillars, 20–30 cm gaps, steps of −0.3 to +0.2 m | up to 4 narrower pillars, gaps up to 1.5 m, steps of −0.8 to +1 m |

The same seed always gives the same course, on every machine. Every piece is
tagged with its obstacle type and section (for the bot's perception later).

[`src/core/parkour_env.h`](src/core/parkour_env.h) wraps a simulation and a course
into a training environment. Progress is the distance along the route of the
furthest point the centre of mass has reached; a run ends when the bot
finishes, breaks a joint, falls (com 0.6 m below the floor), leaves the
corridor, stalls (no new 25 cm in 5 s) or runs out of time (10 s + 1.5 s per
metre). The score is metres reached, plus one point per second left when it
finishes. Building a course takes ~20 ms, so each one is reused for 8 episodes.

## Layout

```
assets/        MJCF models and scenes (humanoid.xml is generated)
tools/         model generators (Python)
src/core/      C++ core: simulation, sensors, injury model
apps/          viewer and self-test executables
scripts/       dependency setup
```

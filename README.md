# Parcour bot

A physically simulated humanoid that learns parkour with reinforcement learning:
stairs, hurdles, balance beams, swing bars, walls with holes and platforms,
assembled into randomised obstacle corridors so the bot generalises instead of
memorising one course.

Physics: [MuJoCo](https://mujoco.org) (C++ API). Training: PPO in PyTorch, on
GitHub's runners (see [Training](#training)).

## Quick start (Linux / WSL2)

```bash
scripts/setup_deps.sh                 # downloads MuJoCo 3.17.0 into third_party/
cmake -S . -B build -G Ninja
cmake --build build
build/parcour_selftest                # physics sanity checks + speed benchmark
build/parcour_viewer                  # interactive 3D viewer on a generated course
```

To watch the newest trained policy (needs the GitHub CLI, `gh`, logged in):

```bash
scripts/fetch_checkpoint.sh           # downloads it into runs/latest/
build/parcour_viewer --policy runs/latest/policy.bin
```

Viewer options: `--policy file` (run a trained policy, on the kind of course it
was trained on), `--seed N`, `--difficulty 0..1`, `--obstacles N`, `--track L`
(no obstacles, L m of flat track), `--section K`
(start at the K-th section), `--overview` (camera on the whole course),
`--vision` (show what the bot perceives). Pass a
scene file instead, e.g. `build/parcour_viewer assets/scene_flat.xml`, to get the
plain floor.

The viewer needs a display (WSLg on Windows 11 works out of the box). Build
without it via `-DPARCOUR_BUILD_VIEWER=OFF`.

## The humanoid

Generated by [`tools/gen_humanoid.py`](tools/gen_humanoid.py) into
`assets/humanoid.xml` (1.75 m, 70 kg; regenerate with `--height` / `--mass`).

* 18 rigid segments (pelvis, abdomen, chest, head, upper arms, forearms, hands,
  thighs, shanks, shoe-wearing feet, toes) plus 5 fingers per hand.
* Segment masses after de Leva (1996), lengths from standard anthropometry.
* 37 motor-driven hinge joints with human ranges of motion (knees and elbows
  bend one way only) and human torque limits, asymmetric where muscles are
  (e.g. knee extension 280 Nm, flexion 150 Nm).
* Each hand has 15 passive finger joints driven by one *grip* tendon actuator.
* 39 actions in [-1, 1]. For training they are target joint angles (0 = standing
  pose, ±1 = the joint limits) that a PD law tracks with torques clamped to the
  human limits, like muscles acting as damped springs; the grips stay torques.
  `ActionMode::kTorque` maps actions onto the torque ranges directly instead.

### Joint injuries

[`src/core/injury.h`](src/core/injury.h) — every joint between two segments
breaks (and the episode ends) when, low-pass filtered over 10 ms,

* the reaction force through the joint, or
* the torque around axes the joint can't rotate about (bending a knee sideways), or
* the torque pushing the joint past its range (hyperextension; over-flexion is
  more tolerated, as soft tissue cushions it in a real body)

exceeds that joint's strength. The thresholds are rough values for an athletic
adult and are tunable.

### Sensors

[`src/core/body_state.h`](src/core/body_state.h) computes at every control step:
whole-body centre of mass, its velocity, total linear and angular momentum; for
each segment its position relative to the com, orientation, angular velocity,
linear momentum and angular momentum about the com; joint angles and
velocities, grip state, foot contact forces, head accelerometer/gyro and joint
stress. `BodyState::flatten` turns this into 438 heading-invariant values.

### Perception of the course

[`src/core/perception.h`](src/core/perception.h) adds what the bot sees:

* **99 rays** from the head in a fan (9 directions from 60° left to 60° right ×
  11 angles from 50° up to 78° down) that turns with the body. Each ray reports
  its distance (up to 10 m), the type of obstacle it hit (floor, stairs, hurdle,
  wall, beam, bars, platform) and the surface normal, i.e. the angle of the
  surface. Rays only see the course, not the bot's own body.
* **The next 3 obstacles**: type, how far ahead they start and end, their
  heading, the floor level before and after, and the boxes of up to 4 key pieces
  (the hurdle, the wall parts around the hole, the beam, the bars, the platform
  tops, the first steps) relative to the body.
* **Route hints**: sideways offset from the centreline, the centreline 1, 2 and
  4 m ahead, distance to the finish and time left.

`ParkourEnv::observe` returns the full policy input: 1652 values (438 body +
1089 rays + 111 obstacles + 14 route). The rays take about 65 µs per control
step, ~4% of the physics cost.

## Obstacle courses

[`src/core/course.h`](src/core/course.h) generates a corridor from a seed and a
difficulty in [0, 1]: a start runway, N obstacle sections and a finish runway.
The corridor turns by up to 25° between sections and floats over an abyss, so
leaving it sideways or missing a jump is a fall.

| Obstacle | Easy (difficulty 0) | Hard (difficulty 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Stairs (up or down) | 2–3 steps, 10–12 cm | up to 8 steps, 10–22 cm |
| Hurdle (board or vault box) | 15–20 cm | up to 1 m high, 0.9 m deep |
| Wall with a hole | hole 1.0–1.2 m wide, 1.2–1.3 m tall, at the floor | hole down to 0.65 × 0.7 m, up to 0.9 m above the floor |
| Balance beam over a pit | 45–60 cm wide, 1.5–2 m long | 12–25 cm wide, up to 5 m long |
| Swing bars over a pit | 1 bar at 2.0 m, 1–1.4 m pit | up to 3 bars at 2.0–2.4 m, pit up to 6.4 m |
| Platforms (pillars with gaps) | 2 pillars, 20–30 cm gaps, steps of −0.3 to +0.2 m | up to 4 narrower pillars, gaps up to 1.5 m, steps of −0.8 to +1 m |

The same seed always gives the same course, on every machine. Every piece is
tagged with its obstacle type and section, which the rays report.

[`src/core/parkour_env.h`](src/core/parkour_env.h) wraps a simulation and a course
into a training environment. Progress is the distance along the route of the
furthest point the centre of mass has reached; a run ends when the bot
finishes, breaks a joint, falls (com 0.6 m below the floor), leaves the
corridor, stalls (no new 25 cm in 5 s) or runs out of time (10 s + 1.5 s per
metre). The score is metres reached, plus one point per second left when it
finishes. Building a course takes ~20 ms, so each one is reused for 8 episodes.

## Training

PPO in PyTorch ([`train/`](train)); the simulation runs in C++ threads through
the Python module `parcour` ([`python/bindings.cpp`](python/bindings.cpp)), with
64 environments stepped in parallel.

* **Network**: separate actor and critic, each: normalised observation → one
  linear block per input part (body 256, rays 64, next obstacles 48, route 16) →
  256 → 128, ELU. The actor outputs the mean of a Gaussian over the 39 target
  angles, with a learned spread per joint.
* **PPO**: 64 envs × 64 steps per update, 5 epochs, 4 minibatches, γ = 0.99,
  GAE λ = 0.95, learning rate adapted to keep the policy change (KL) near 0.01,
  rewards scaled by the running spread of returns, time-limit endings
  bootstrapped.
* **Stages** ([`train/stages.py`](train/stages.py)), advanced automatically when
  the goal holds over the last 300 episodes:

  | Stage | Task | Reward | Goal |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | stand | stay up for 10 s on a flat track | 1 per second alive | 90% of the time survived |
  | walk | reach the end of 30 m within 45 s | 1 per metre, 0.5 per second, 5 at the finish | 70% finish |
  | run | 30 m within 25 s | 1 per metre, 1 per second left at the finish | 70% finish at 3 m/s |

  A run also ends when the com drops below 0.75 / 0.65 / 0.6 m above the ground
  ("fell over"). The obstacle stages follow in step 5.

**On GitHub** ([`.github/workflows/train.yml`](.github/workflows/train.yml)):
Actions → *train* → *Run workflow* (or `gh workflow run train.yml`). A round
trains 3 copies in parallel for ~5.3 h from the newest checkpoint, keeps the
best one as the artifact `checkpoint` and starts the next round, 4 rounds by
default. The run page shows the stage, evaluation and learning curve. Cancel a
running round to stop.

**Locally**: `python3 train/train.py --minutes 60 --out runs/a` (add
`--resume runs/a` to continue). It writes `ckpt.pt`, `policy.bin` for the
viewer, `metrics.csv`, `eval.json` and `summary.md`. `train/test_train.py` is
a one-minute check of the whole pipeline.

## Layout

```
assets/        MJCF models and scenes (humanoid.xml is generated)
tools/         model generators (Python)
src/core/      C++ core: simulation, sensors, injury model, courses, perception, env,
               batched env (vec_env), trained policy inference (policy)
python/        Python module for training (pybind11)
train/         PPO trainer, stages, tests
apps/          viewer and self-test executables
scripts/       dependency setup, checkpoint download
```
